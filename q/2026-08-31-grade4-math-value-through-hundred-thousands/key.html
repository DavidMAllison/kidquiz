Grade 4 Math Practice - Place Value Through Hundred Thousands — Answer Key

Grade 4 · Math · ~30 minutes · August 31, 2026
Students use a place-value chart, expanded form (sum of place values), expanded notation (multiplication expressions), left-to-right digit comparison, and the round-by-place-value rule.
1. 20,000
  1. Place the digits in a place-value chart: 5 hundred thousands, 2 ten thousands, 8 thousands, 3 hundreds, 4 tens, 0 ones.
  2. The 2 sits in the ten thousands column.
  3. Value = 2 x 10,000 = 20,000.
2. 400,000 + 7,000 + 200 + 10 + 5
  1. Write each digit with its place: 4 hundred thousands, 0 ten thousands, 7 thousands, 2 hundreds, 1 ten, 5 ones.
  2. Write the value of each nonzero digit: 400,000, 7,000, 200, 10, 5.
  3. Add them as a sum of place values: 400,000 + 7,000 + 200 + 10 + 5.
3. (6 x 100,000) + (8 x 10,000) + (3 x 1,000) + (0 x 100) + (7 x 10) + (9 x 1)
  1. Line up digits with places: 6 hundred thousands, 8 ten thousands, 3 thousands, 0 hundreds, 7 tens, 9 ones.
  2. Multiply each digit by its place value.
  3. Join the products with plus signs: (6 x 100,000) + (8 x 10,000) + (3 x 1,000) + (0 x 100) + (7 x 10) + (9 x 1).
4. 216,504
  1. 'Two hundred sixteen thousand' fills the thousands period: 216 thousands.
  2. 'Five hundred four' fills the last three places: 5 hundreds, 0 tens, 4 ones.
  3. Standard form: 216,504.
5. three hundred fifty thousand, sixty-two
  1. Split the number into periods: 350 | 062.
  2. Read the thousands period: three hundred fifty thousand.
  3. Read the last three digits: sixty-two (0 hundreds, so it is not named).
  4. Word form: three hundred fifty thousand, sixty-two.
6. hundred thousands 7, ten thousands 9, thousands 4, hundreds 1, tens 8, ones 3
  1. Write 794,183 into the chart columns from the right: 3 ones, 8 tens, 1 hundred.
  2. Continue left: 4 thousands, 9 ten thousands, 7 hundred thousands.
  3. Read the chart back to fill the blanks.
7. >
  1. Compare digit by digit from the left: 4 = 4, 6 = 6, 2 = 2.
  2. Next place is hundreds: 9 vs 1. Since 9 > 1, 462,910 is greater.
  3. So 462,910 > 462,190.
8. 570,000
  1. Find the ten thousands place: the 7 in 574,628.
  2. Look at the digit to its right (thousands place): 4.
  3. Since 4 is less than 5, round down and keep the 7; all places to the right become 0.
  4. Answer: 570,000.
9. Nearest hundred thousand: 200,000; nearest thousand: 238,000
  1. Nearest hundred thousand: the place digit is 2; the digit to its right (ten thousands) is 3, which is less than 5, so round down to 200,000.
  2. Nearest thousand: the place digit is 8; the digit to its right (hundreds) is 4, which is less than 5, so round down and keep 238, giving 238,000.
10. 0, 1, 2, or 3
  1. Compare from the left: hundred thousands are both 8, so move right.
  2. The ten thousands place must make the left number smaller than 4 ten thousands.
  3. If the box is 4, the numbers would be equal, so the box must be less than 4.
  4. Digits that work: 0, 1, 2, 3.
11. True
  1. The 6 in the ten thousands place has value 60,000; the 6 in the thousands place has value 6,000.
  2. 60,000 = 10 x 6,000, so the statement is true.
12. About 316,000 visitors
  1. Find the thousands place in 315,749: the 5.
  2. Look at the digit to its right (hundreds place): 7.
  3. Since 7 is 5 or greater, round up: 5 thousands becomes 6 thousands.
  4. All places to the right become 0: 316,000.
13. The second concert, with 128,560 people, had more.
  1. Compare left to right: 1 = 1, 2 = 2, 8 = 8, 5 = 5.
  2. In the tens place, 6 > 0, so 128,560 > 128,506.
  3. The concert with 128,560 people had more attendance.
14. She is not correct. The correct expanded form is 90,000 + 4,000 + 600 + 7.
  1. Place 94,607 in a chart: 9 ten thousands, 4 thousands, 6 hundreds, 0 tens, 7 ones.
  2. Mira wrote 60 instead of 600, so she used the wrong place for the 6.
  3. Correct expanded form: 90,000 + 4,000 + 600 + 7.
