Grade 4 Math Practice - Place Value, Number Forms, Comparing, and Rounding — Answer Key

Grade 4 · Math · ~25 minutes · August 31, 2026
Students use place-value charts, expanded form and expanded notation, digit-by-digit comparing with >, < and =, and the 'find the place, look at the digit to its right' rounding rule.
1. hundred thousands
  1. Write 526,914 in a place-value chart from the right: 4 ones, 1 ten, 9 hundreds, 6 thousands, 2 ten thousands, 5 hundred thousands.
  2. The 5 sits in the sixth place from the right, which is the hundred thousands place.
2. six hundred eighty-three thousand, fifty-nine
  1. Split the number at the comma: 683 thousands and 059.
  2. Read the thousands period: six hundred eighty-three thousand.
  3. Read the last three digits: fifty-nine. So the word form is six hundred eighty-three thousand, fifty-nine.
3. 215,406
  1. Place 'two hundred fifteen thousand' in the thousands period of the chart: 2 hundred thousands, 1 ten thousand, 5 thousands.
  2. Place 'four hundred six' in the last three places: 4 hundreds, 0 tens, 6 ones.
  3. Read the chart across: 215,406.
4. 300,000 + 90,000 + 1,000 + 200 + 4
  1. Find the value of each digit: 3 = 300,000; 9 = 90,000; 1 = 1,000; 2 = 200; 0 tens = 0; 4 = 4.
  2. Add the values that are not zero: 300,000 + 90,000 + 1,000 + 200 + 4.
5. (6 x 100,000) + (4 x 1,000) + (5 x 100) + (3 x 10)
  1. Name each digit's place: 6 hundred thousands, 0 ten thousands, 4 thousands, 5 hundreds, 3 tens, 0 ones.
  2. Multiply each digit by its place value: 6 x 100,000, 4 x 1,000, 5 x 100, 3 x 10.
  3. Skip the zeros and add the parts: (6 x 100,000) + (4 x 1,000) + (5 x 100) + (3 x 10).
6. 70,000
  1. In 275,318 the digit 7 is the second place from the left, the ten thousands place.
  2. Multiply the digit by its place value: 7 x 10,000 = 70,000.
7. (a) 452,013 - 400,000; (b) 145,203 - 40,000; (c) 214,530 - 4,000; (d) 213,045 - 40
  1. In 452,013 the 4 is in the hundred thousands place: 4 x 100,000 = 400,000.
  2. In 145,203 the 4 is in the ten thousands place: 4 x 10,000 = 40,000.
  3. In 214,530 the 4 is in the thousands place: 4 x 1,000 = 4,000.
  4. In 213,045 the 4 is in the tens place: 4 x 10 = 40.
8. >
  1. Compare digit by digit from the left: hundred thousands 3 = 3, ten thousands 8 = 8, thousands 6 = 6, hundreds 1 = 1.
  2. In the tens place, 7 is greater than 5, so 386,175 > 386,157.
9. <
  1. Count digits: 92,480 has 5 digits and 192,480 has 6 digits.
  2. A 6-digit number is greater than a 5-digit number, so 92,480 < 192,480.
10. True
  1. Both numbers have 5 hundred thousands, so compare the next place.
  2. 530,000 has 3 ten thousands and 503,000 has 0 ten thousands, so 530,000 is greater. The statement is true.
11. (a) 674,000 (b) 670,000 (c) 700,000
  1. (a) Nearest thousand: the thousands digit is 4. Look at the hundreds digit, 3. Since 3 < 5, round down: 674,000.
  2. (b) Nearest ten thousand: the ten thousands digit is 7. Look at the thousands digit, 4. Since 4 < 5, round down: 670,000.
  3. (c) Nearest hundred thousand: the hundred thousands digit is 6. Look at the ten thousands digit, 7. Since 7 is 5 or more, round up: 700,000.
12. 846,500
  1. To round to the nearest ten thousand, look at the thousands digit of each choice.
  2. 843,299: thousands digit 3, rounds down to 840,000.
  3. 846,500: thousands digit 6, rounds up to 850,000. This is the answer.
  4. 858,120 rounds to 860,000 and 862,000 rounds to 860,000.
13. The second library has more books, because 268,704 > 268,470.
  1. Compare digit by digit from the left: 2 = 2, 6 = 6, 8 = 8, and the hundreds digits are 4 and 7.
  2. Since 7 hundreds is greater than 4 hundreds, 268,704 is the larger number, so the second library has more books.
14. The 4 in the ten thousands place is worth 40,000 and the 4 in the thousands place is worth 4,000, so it is 10 times greater.
  1. The first 4 is in the ten thousands place: 4 x 10,000 = 40,000.
  2. The second 4 is in the thousands place: 4 x 1,000 = 4,000.
  3. Because 40,000 = 10 x 4,000, a digit one place to the left is always 10 times the value of the same digit to its right.
